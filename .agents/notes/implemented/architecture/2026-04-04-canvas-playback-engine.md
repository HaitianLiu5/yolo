# Agent Note: Render to canvas, not to a video element

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-04-04-canvas-playback-engine.zh.md)

## Problem

Tesla's browser can freeze a native `<video>` element while the vehicle is moving, which defeats an in-car player (for passengers, or while parked). The player must decode and present frames itself, keep picture and sound in sync without the browser's media pipeline, and stay within the memory of a car's browser on a mobile connection.

## Decision

In car mode the client decodes media with Mediabunny and WebCodecs and draws each frame to a `<canvas>` ([`clients/player/src/engine/mediabunny-player.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/mediabunny-player.js) and its helpers in the same folder). The car never plays through `<video>`. Yolo Web, the same app in a computer's or phone's browser, plays through the browser's own `<video>` element instead, behind the same engine interface ([two engines](2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md)).

- **The audio clock.** Decoded PCM goes through a lock-free ring buffer on a `SharedArrayBuffer` with `Atomics` ([`audio-ring-buffer.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/audio-ring-buffer.js)): the main thread writes samples, an AudioWorklet reads and plays them, and the number of samples played is the clock for the whole player. Because the clock counts samples, each decoded buffer is placed by its timestamp ([`audio-timeline.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/audio-timeline.js)): a gap of more than 20 ms becomes silence, an overlap is trimmed, a jump of more than 10 seconds re-anchors without silence, and the engine counts these as `audioDiscontinuities`. A stream without audio, or one whose audio ends early, continues on a wall clock from the same position. The output opens at the rate and channel count the decoder gives where playback starts, not the ones the container states: HE-AAC with implicit SBR states AAC-LC at half its rate, as Tencent Video's 24 kHz ADTS headers do for sound that decodes to 48 kHz, and HE-AAC v2 states mono for stereo. Until 2026-11-01 the output opened at the stated rate, and such sound played in the car at half speed, an octave down. A buffer that comes at another rate later fails the stream (`DECODER_FAILED`), and the new stream opens the output at the rate there.
- **Playback speed.** At a speed other than 1 ([two engines](2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md#playback-speed)) the AudioWorklet stretches the sound in time and keeps its pitch, as a `<video>` element does ([`audio-worklet-processor.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/audio-worklet-processor.js)): WSOLA, with Chromium's sizes, overlap-adds 20 ms windows, each placed within 15 ms of where the speed puts it, where the sound after the last window continues best. It still counts the media frames it played, so the clock, and the picture, subtitles, and danmaku with it, follow the speed with no change of their own; the wall clock runs at the speed when there is no sound, and the picture allows for the sound on its way to the speakers at the speed. At 1 the worklet copies the ring as before. The search costs most of the stretch, about 10 ms of the audio thread a second (1% of one core) against 0.35 ms at 1, measured in Node on an Apple-silicon Mac on 2026-10-21.
- **Cross-origin isolation.** Browsers allow `SharedArrayBuffer` only on cross-origin isolated pages, so the page carries `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: credentialless`: in production from [`clients/player/public/_headers`](../../../../clients/player/public/_headers), in development from `clients/player/vite.config.js`. Without them `load()` in `mediabunny-player.js` throws; there is no fallback. They stay on every page of the app, also where Yolo Web plays through `<video>`, because the page learns its mode only after it loads. Because the client is on the same origin as `/m/*` and `/v1/realtime`, media responses need no CORP ([same-origin passthrough](2026-10-07-same-origin-media-passthrough.md)); the one other media origin, the node's LAN listener, answers with CORS and `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: cross-origin` ([LAN direct play](../feature/2026-10-12-lan-direct-play.md)).
- **Bounded memory.** The decoded video queue holds 6 to 16 frames, sized for about 0.2 seconds; above normal speed it and the decoded start and rebuffer margins hold that much more media, so they absorb the same jitter in wall time, within the same 16 frames. Network jitter is absorbed by buffering encoded segments instead: the HLS prefetcher keeps up to 210 seconds ahead within 128 MiB. It measures how far a segment lies ahead in seconds from its own playlist's read position, not on a clock the playlists share. A seek moves that read position to the segment that holds the target on the timeline Mediabunny reads: without a date, a playlist that begins past sequence 0, such as a Plex transcode resumed part way, begins on it at its media sequence times its target duration. Until 2026-10-21 the read-ahead counted such a playlist from 0, so a seek in it fetched minutes ahead of the segments playback read. At the cap it makes room for the next segment from what playback has passed, or from segments that lie farther ahead than that one, never from the nearer part of its window: trading those for a farther segment only made the next read fetch them again. It downloads in the same order, so audio and video advance together, and reserves for each download the size of its playlist's largest recent segment.
- **A short start.** Playback starts, and plays on after a seek, once 3 seconds of encoded media are buffered, and the prefetcher keeps filling toward its target while the picture moves ([`playback-buffer-policy.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/playback-buffer-policy.js)). A stall with media still buffered, such as a decoder hiccup, resumes as soon as frames are decoded; a stall that emptied the network buffer waits for 5 seconds, so a link slower than the stream does not stall again at once.
- **The audio device opens early.** The first AudioContext a page makes opens the system's audio output, which holds the page for up to 2 seconds (1.8 seconds in Chrome on macOS). The page's first tap opens it once the tap's own handlers have run ([`audio-output.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/audio-output.js)): a tap that plays sends its request first, and the device opens while Yolo prepares the stream. The `<video>` engine needs no AudioContext.
- **Single-flight prefetch.** The HLS prefetcher fetches one segment at a time, so background buffering never competes with the segment the player needs now. A read of a buffered segment gets what it asked for: the range of a `Range` request, as a 206, or the whole segment, as a 200. Mediabunny places a 206 body at the offset it asked for, so the whole segment for an offset past 0 would put its first bytes where later ones belong.
- **Position is preserved.** Changing quality or viewport replaces the stream at the current position. While a stream loads, the engine reports the position it is about to start at, as the `<video>` engine does, so a report, a recovery, or a quality change asked for meanwhile goes on from where the screen was.
- **Bounded retries.** A segment that keeps failing ends the stream rather than being retried forever. The HLS prefetcher retries a segment a bounded number of times. Mediabunny then gives up at once on a 403, a 410, or a segment whose tries ran out, and after three more tries on anything else, such as a network error or a download that a seek cancelled. `player.js` recovers with a fresh stream at the same position, through the tunnel after a LAN failure; a source that answers 403 is shown as an error. Only the 403 of a request playback makes counts: a 401 or 403 to a read-ahead, which can be 210 seconds early, waits for the segment's next retry cycle like any other failure, and playback that reaches the segment first asks for it itself ([a refused read-ahead](../bug-fix/2026-10-16-refused-read-ahead.md)).
- **A failed decoder recovers in place.** A decoder error does not replace the stream: the track whose decoder failed climbs a ladder on what the engine already holds, and the other track plays on ([`decoder-recovery.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/decoder-recovery.js), proposed in [smooth playback first](../../proposed/architecture/2026-10-21-smooth-playback-first.md)). A failure is a decoder's when the decoder itself reported it or WebCodecs named it (`EncodingError`, `DataError`, `OperationError`); a 410, a 403, or a segment whose tries ran out keeps the player's recovery above. The place it failed is the failed decoder's last packet. (1) **Restart**: a new decoder of that track from the end of what is decoded, on the buffered segments, so nothing is fetched; while the picture's decoder restarts, the sound plays on over the last picture for up to 2 seconds before the engine waits for both as for any stall. (2) **Refetch and software**, if the track fails again before it decodes past that place: the segment that holds it leaves the buffer, a new Mediabunny input reads it again from the same session through the same prefetcher, and the picture decodes with `hardwareAcceleration: "prefer-software"` up to the next key frame, where the hardware decoder takes over again. (3) **Skip**, if it fails again: the track goes on from the next key frame after that place (for sound, the next packet); the sound plays while the picture holds until then. (4) Only then the engine fails with `DECODER_FAILED`, and the player asks for a new stream at the position through its recovery and backoff. A step whose new decoder gives nothing out within 4, 15, or 8 seconds of being asked has failed too, a fourth ladder within 10 minutes goes straight to step 4, and a seek leaves the ladder behind. Each step's end is a `decoder_recovery` event ([device telemetry](../feature/2026-10-11-device-telemetry.md)). On 2026-10-21, in the built player at the car's window size, on a 720p stream at 2200 kbps in 8-second segments, on an Apple-silicon Mac with Chrome's CPU throttling from 1 to 10 times, the restarted decoder gave its first new frame 20 to 210 ms after it was asked and decoded past the failed place 330 to 570 ms after the failure while the frames decoded before it played on, the picture never waited more than 117 ms for a frame (33 to 67 ms without a failure), and playback never stalled.
- **Faults for tests and in-car checks.** Opened with `?decodeErrorAt=<seconds>`, the player's canvas engine makes its decoders fail at that media time as a broken hardware decoder does, `decodeErrorCount` times (default 1, counted over every stream the page plays) on the `decodeErrorTrack` (`video`, the default, or `audio`); `?videoStallMs=<ms>` with `videoStallAt` holds the picture's decoder instead. The player reads them once, from the address the page opened at. On a playing page, `__yoloPlayer.player.injectDecodeError(seconds, { count, track })` and `injectVideoStall(ms)` do the same for the engine on screen.

## Alternatives considered

**A native `<video>` element.** It freezes while the vehicle moves, which is the problem the project exists to solve.

**A 15-second start buffer**, the engine's first rule, for every start, seek, and stall. On a 720p stream over a link of 10 Mbit/s it kept a still picture for 5 seconds after the first frame was decoded, and for 20 seconds or more where the link barely carried the stream, long enough for [adaptive quality](../feature/2026-10-11-adaptive-quality.md) to read the wait as a stall and rebuild the stream before it ever played (measured on 2026-10-14). The 210-second target, not the start threshold, is what carries the car through a dead spot.

**libmedia's AVPlayer** (WebAssembly decoding with WebGL rendering), which the player used first and which also needed `SharedArrayBuffer`. Mediabunny with the browser's WebCodecs decoders replaced it on 2026-07-13; the change records no further reason.

## Consequences

Playback works while the car moves, at the price of owning what a `<video>` element would do: demuxing, audio output, A/V sync, buffering, and recovery. Recovering a decoder in place keeps the buffer, at the cost of more logic in the engine, a second Mediabunny input for a track after a refetch (at most one per track), and software decoding of up to one group of pictures, which the car's processor has not been measured doing. Cross-origin isolation is permanent: COOP cuts the link between the page and any popup it opens, so the player page cannot sign in through a popup, and screens pair by QR code instead ([device code pairing](../feature/2026-10-07-device-code-pairing.md)). Every media response must come from the player's origin or carry CORS and CORP headers, which only the node's LAN listener does ([LAN direct play](../feature/2026-10-12-lan-direct-play.md)). Removing the COOP/COEP headers, or moving the client to another origin than `/m/*`, breaks playback outright. A faster speed costs the car what it would cost any player: at 2 the decoder decodes twice the frames a second, the stream needs twice its bitrate, and the encoded buffer's 210 seconds carry the car through half as long a dead spot; whether the car's decoder keeps a 60 fps stream at 2 has not been measured in the car.
