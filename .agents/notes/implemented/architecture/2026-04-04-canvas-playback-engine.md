# Agent Note: Render to canvas, not to a video element

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-04-04-canvas-playback-engine.zh.md)

## Problem

Tesla's browser can freeze a native `<video>` element while the vehicle is moving, which defeats an in-car player (for passengers, or while parked). The player must decode and present frames itself, keep picture and sound in sync without the browser's media pipeline, and stay within the memory of a car's browser on a mobile connection.

## Decision

In the car's profile the client decodes media with Mediabunny and WebCodecs and draws each frame to a `<canvas>` ([`clients/player/src/engine/mediabunny-player.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/mediabunny-player.js) and its helpers in the same folder). The car never plays through `<video>`. Yolo Web, the same app in a computer's or phone's browser, plays through the browser's own `<video>` element instead, behind the same engine interface ([two engines](2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md)).

- **The audio clock.** Decoded PCM goes through a lock-free ring buffer on a `SharedArrayBuffer` with `Atomics` ([`audio-ring-buffer.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/audio-ring-buffer.js)): the main thread writes samples, an AudioWorklet reads and plays them, and the number of samples played is the clock for the whole player. Because the clock counts samples, each decoded buffer is placed by its timestamp ([`audio-timeline.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/audio-timeline.js)): a gap of more than 20 ms becomes silence, an overlap is trimmed, a jump of more than 10 seconds re-anchors without silence, and the engine counts these as `audioDiscontinuities`. A stream without audio, or one whose audio ends early, continues on a wall clock from the same position.
- **Cross-origin isolation.** Browsers allow `SharedArrayBuffer` only on cross-origin isolated pages, so the page carries `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: credentialless`: in production from [`clients/player/public/_headers`](../../../../clients/player/public/_headers), in development from `clients/player/vite.config.js`. Without them `load()` in `mediabunny-player.js` throws; there is no fallback. They stay on every page of the app, also where Yolo Web plays through `<video>`, because the page learns its profile only after it loads. Because the client is on the same origin as `/m/*` and `/v1/realtime`, media responses need no CORP ([same-origin passthrough](2026-10-07-same-origin-media-passthrough.md)); the one other media origin, the node's LAN listener, answers with CORS and `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: cross-origin` ([LAN direct play](../feature/2026-10-12-lan-direct-play.md)).
- **Bounded memory.** The decoded video queue holds 6 to 16 frames, sized for about 0.2 seconds. Network jitter is absorbed by buffering encoded segments instead: the HLS prefetcher keeps up to 210 seconds ahead within 128 MiB. It measures how far a segment lies ahead in seconds from its own playlist's read position, not on a clock the playlists share. At the cap it makes room for the next segment from what playback has passed, or from segments that lie farther ahead than that one, never from the nearer part of its window: trading those for a farther segment only made the next read fetch them again. It downloads in the same order, so audio and video advance together, and reserves for each download the size of its playlist's largest recent segment.
- **A short start.** Playback starts, and plays on after a seek, once 3 seconds of encoded media are buffered, and the prefetcher keeps filling toward its target while the picture moves ([`playback-buffer-policy.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/engine/playback-buffer-policy.js)). A stall with media still buffered, such as a decoder hiccup, resumes as soon as frames are decoded; a stall that emptied the network buffer waits for 5 seconds, so a link slower than the stream does not stall again at once.
- **The audio device opens early.** The first AudioContext a page makes opens the system's audio output, which holds the page for up to 2 seconds (1.8 seconds in Chrome on macOS). The page's first tap opens it once the tap's own handlers have run ([`audio-output.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/audio-output.js)): a tap that plays sends its request first, and the device opens while Yolo prepares the stream. The `<video>` engine needs no AudioContext.
- **Single-flight prefetch.** The HLS prefetcher fetches one segment at a time, so background buffering never competes with the segment the player needs now.
- **Position is preserved.** Changing quality or viewport replaces the stream at the current position.
- **Bounded retries.** A segment that keeps failing ends the stream rather than being retried forever. The HLS prefetcher retries a segment a bounded number of times. Mediabunny then gives up at once on a 403, a 410, or a segment whose tries ran out, and after three more tries on anything else, such as a network error or a download that a seek cancelled. `player.js` recovers with a fresh stream at the same position, through the tunnel after a LAN failure; a source that answers 403 is shown as an error. Only the 403 of a request playback makes counts: a 401 or 403 to a read-ahead, which can be 210 seconds early, waits for the segment's next retry cycle like any other failure, and playback that reaches the segment first asks for it itself ([a refused read-ahead](../bug-fix/2026-10-16-refused-read-ahead.md)).

## Alternatives considered

**A native `<video>` element.** It freezes while the vehicle moves, which is the problem the project exists to solve.

**A 15-second start buffer**, the engine's first rule, for every start, seek, and stall. On a 720p stream over a link of 10 Mbit/s it kept a still picture for 5 seconds after the first frame was decoded, and for 20 seconds or more where the link barely carried the stream, long enough for [adaptive quality](../feature/2026-10-11-adaptive-quality.md) to read the wait as a stall and rebuild the stream before it ever played (measured on 2026-10-14). The 210-second target, not the start threshold, is what carries the car through a dead spot.

**libmedia's AVPlayer** (WebAssembly decoding with WebGL rendering), which the player used first and which also needed `SharedArrayBuffer`. Mediabunny with the browser's WebCodecs decoders replaced it on 2026-07-13; the change records no further reason.

## Consequences

Playback works while the car moves, at the price of owning what a `<video>` element would do: demuxing, audio output, A/V sync, buffering, and recovery. Cross-origin isolation is permanent: COOP cuts the link between the page and any popup it opens, so the player page cannot sign in through a popup, and screens pair by QR code instead ([device code pairing](../feature/2026-10-07-device-code-pairing.md)). Every media response must come from the player's origin or carry CORS and CORP headers, which only the node's LAN listener does ([LAN direct play](../feature/2026-10-12-lan-direct-play.md)). Removing the COOP/COEP headers, or moving the client to another origin than `/m/*`, breaks playback outright.
