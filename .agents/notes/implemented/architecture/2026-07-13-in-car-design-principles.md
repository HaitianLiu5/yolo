# Agent Note: In-car design principles

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-07-13-in-car-design-principles.zh.md)

## Problem

The player is used first by occupants on a large in-car touchscreen (its car profile) and also in a computer's or phone's browser (its web profile), and the owner's web pages sit beside it. The main job is to start and control media with as little typing and precise tapping as possible, and the controls must also work from a phone-sized browser. Without a stated direction, each screen drifts toward a developer dashboard or a generic streaming-app clone.

## Decision

Every screen follows one design context:

- **Personality:** calm, direct, cinematic. It should feel dependable and purpose-built for a car, not like a developer dashboard or a generic streaming clone.
- **Aesthetic direction:** a restrained black interface with high contrast, large touch targets, and sparse typography. Functional state matters more than decoration. Avoid neon effects, busy cards, tiny controls, and modal-heavy settings.
- **Principles:**
  - Prefer one-tap choices over typed configuration.
  - Make the current state visible before the user acts.
  - Use touch targets of at least 48 px (`--touch`) for primary in-car controls.
  - Keep playback controls visually quiet until needed.
  - Preserve a stable layout across Tesla and phone viewports.

The palette, spacing, type sizes, radii, and touch sizes are CSS custom properties in [`clients/player/src/style.css`](../../../../clients/player/src/style.css) that every screen shares: a neutral `oklch` palette on pure black, a 4 px spacing scale up to 48 px, type from 13 to 40 px, and frosted menus. A list that scrolls shows no scrollbar; an edge with more behind it fades out and blurs a little instead, across the home screen's rows and down the settings sheet ([`scroll-edges.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/scroll-edges.js) marks the edges). Every control uses the 48 px touch target (`--touch`) with 22 px icons; at the car's default display size that is about 60 physical pixels. The settings sheet is the exception: settings are changed parked, never while driving, so it takes a phone's settings density, 40 px targets in 48 px rows, in the car too. Errors the person watching sees are a single sentence with **Try again** and **Back**, or only **Back** when trying again cannot play the video, never technical detail ([device telemetry](../feature/2026-10-11-device-telemetry.md)). Waiting has one look on every screen: the launch mark while a start has nothing to show yet, skeletons in the placeholder tone where items are on their way, which shimmer card after card in a computer's or phone's browser and hold still in the car (a moving page is never idle, and the car would draw every frame), and the item's own picture under the loading ring from a tap until it plays. No screen says "Connecting…" or "Loading…" ([Yolo Web](../feature/2026-10-11-yolo-web-client.md#starting-up)).

The car's browser window ends above the car's app dock: the window is 601 CSS pixels (920 physical pixels) tall, and Tesla's status bar, the browser's toolbar, and the dock take the rest of the 1200-pixel panel. So the layout keeps no band for the dock, only the bottom edge's own safe-area inset (`--safe-bottom`, a phone's home indicator). The player centres the picture in the whole window, not in the space below the top bar, because the top bar hides with the controls and the picture would sit low while watching. Its progress bar and controls sit on the window's bottom edge, which puts them in the band below a 16:9 picture, and the top bar in the band above it; menus fit between the two.

The player's controls follow the YouTube app on a phone or tablet, because the car's screen is a touch screen ([`controls.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/controls.js)). A tap on the picture shows one overlay with no scrim over the picture, so its brightness stays: the pills and the centre buttons carry their own translucent fills, the title and the Back arrow cast a shadow, and the progress bar's unplayed part is YouTube's translucent white, so it shows over a dark picture too; another tap, 3 seconds of playing, or a mouse leaving the player hides it. A pause brings it up, whoever asked for it (the owner, an agent, or the system's controls), and while paused it stays; waiting never does, because a stall is not a pause: a stall, a seek's or a quality change's stream, a recovery, and the first load leave it as it was, hidden or hiding on its usual 3 seconds. While anything loads, one small loading ring shows over the picture's centre, whether or not the overlay does: a thin ring, half of it lit in soft white with a faint dark halo so it holds over a bright picture, with no disc behind it, 28 pixels across (32 in the car, for the distance); it fades in only once the wait has lasted half a second, so a short one shows nothing, and with the overlay it takes the place of the play button's sign. Without motion it stands still and its arc brightens and dims. A tap never pauses by itself, and on a car's screen every press counts as a touch, whatever pointer the browser reports. The overlay holds the top bar (Back and the title), previous, play/pause, and next over the picture's centre (previous when Yolo's history has an earlier item, next when the queue has one or previous came back from one; [the Hub](2026-10-07-hub-and-realtime-channel.md#previous-and-next)), and on the bottom edge YouTube's desktop bar: the progress bar across, and under it every other control on the left, where a driver in a left-hand-drive car reaches: one pill with play/pause, volume, and the time, and one with the danmaku, subtitle (CC), audio, speed, settings, and fullscreen buttons, audio only when there is a track to choose and speed except on a live stream. The speed button opens a panel: the speed, a slider from 0.5× to the engine's fastest (5× on the web, 3× in the car) on which 0.5× to 2× takes half the web's track, and the presets 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, and 3; the button shows the speed in place of its gauge while it is not 1× ([two engines](2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md#playback-speed)). Each menu opens above its button, centred on it and kept inside the gutters. With a mouse (the web profile, where the browser's pointer hovers and is fine; never in the car), the player is YouTube's desktop one: no centre row, previous and next beside play/pause in the first pill, each only when there is one, and each button's name over it. There is no Picture-in-Picture button. A double tap on the left or right third seeks 10 seconds back or ahead, 10 more for each further tap, with YouTube's ripple and without showing the overlay; a press and hold plays at 2× while held and goes back to the chosen speed when it lets go, and does nothing at 2× or faster; and a swipe down is Back. Danmaku stays under the overlay, frozen while paused; subtitles take YouTube's size and place, 16 pixels for every 360 of the picture's height (at least 16) and 2% of its height above its bottom edge, and stay just above the bottom row while it shows. The pointer hides with the controls; while a stream loads, a mouse's movement shows it alone. On a phone held upright, as in the YouTube app, the top bar and the bottom row lie on the picture itself, the centre row alone plays and pauses, and while they show, subtitles move into the band below the picture. Where the row is too narrow for every control (640 CSS pixels or less), the second pill scrolls sideways and its edges fade, so every button stays a full touch target and none is cut off. With a mouse, a click plays or pauses, and Space or K, J and L, the arrow keys, F, M, and Shift+. and Shift+, (a quarter faster and slower) work as on YouTube.

A bilingual subtitle cue shows one line per language ([`subtitle-layout.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/subtitle-layout.js)): its lines' languages are told apart by script (Chinese, Japanese, Korean, or Latin letters), and consecutive lines in one language join, Chinese and Japanese without a space, so a source that breaks its Chinese in two still shows one English line and one Chinese line. A cue in one language keeps its author's lines, which may be two speakers, or two languages that share a script. Each line keeps one row where it can: one wider than the subtitle area (92% of the picture's width) shrinks to fit, down to three quarters of its size; one wider still wraps into the fewest rows it fills at that size, at the largest size that fills them. [`subtitles.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/subtitles.js) measures the lines when they change and when the area's size or the fonts do, never per frame, since the canvas engine draws on the same thread and a long task drops a frame. Each row's background follows its own text, as YouTube's does, rather than spanning the area. The system's player on iPhone gets the same lines.

The same tokens serve [Yolo Web](../feature/2026-10-11-yolo-web-client.md) in a computer's or phone's browser and the owner's web pages, which add a light scheme that follows the system setting ([owner web pages](2026-10-12-owner-web-pages.md)); the Yolo mark and the device types' icons come from one module. Lists, sheets, and pages carry their structure with spacing, grouping, and type rather than borders and divider lines, keep one left edge with actions on the right, and say only the essential sentence. Settings are one sheet whose destructive actions confirm in place, never a dialog over a dialog; every pressable control moves when pressed, and the sheet works with a keyboard as well as a touchscreen.

## Consequences

Screens share one visual vocabulary through the tokens, so a new view inherits the palette, spacing, and touch sizes instead of inventing its own. A [display size](../feature/2026-10-11-display-size.md) other than native scales every CSS pixel, so touch targets shrink with the chosen size.
