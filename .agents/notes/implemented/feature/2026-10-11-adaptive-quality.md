# Agent Note: Adapt quality: a lower rate replaces the stream, a higher one waits for the next

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-10-11-adaptive-quality.zh.md)

## Problem

A car's mobile connection varies from strong to barely usable within one drive. A fixed quality either stalls on a weak connection or wastes a strong one. The canvas engine cannot switch variants inside a stream the way a native HLS player does: it plays one rendition, so any quality change means a new stream, which costs a short pause and throws away the encoded segments buffered ahead, up to 210 seconds in the car.

## Decision

The client measures throughput and decides the screen's bitrate cap ([`clients/player/src/quality.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/quality.js)), with either playback engine ([two engines](../architecture/2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md)). The canvas engine measures with its HLS prefetcher; the `<video>` engine with hls.js's bandwidth estimate or, without hls.js, by how fast the buffer grows while the browser fetches. A step down is a new stream now: `POST /v1/playback/play` with `reason: "quality"`, the current position, the `sessionId` it replaces, and `maxVideoKbps`. A step up never replaces the stream that plays and its buffer: it becomes the rate the screen's next stream on that media path starts at, which Yolo keeps for the screen and path. [Smooth playback first](../../proposed/architecture/2026-10-21-smooth-playback-first.md) holds the rationale. The policy is quick only when playback already suffers:

- **Down:** when throughput stays below the stream's bitrate × 1.15 while the player is stalled for 8 seconds, or while a buffer under 20 seconds drains for 20 seconds. The new cap is 75% of the measured throughput minus audio, rounded down to a rung of the ladder 400, 700, 1000, 1500, 2200, 3000, 4000, 6000, 9000, 13000 kbps, at least 15% below what plays; the stream is rebuilt at it. The rungs above 4000 serve the direct paths, whose ceilings are higher than the relay's: a stall there need not drop to 4 Mbps. A second step down waits 20 seconds.
- **Up:** one rung, after throughput has stayed at or above the next rung's bitrate × 1.6 with at least 30 seconds of buffer for 90 seconds, and not in a stream's first 90 seconds. Nothing is rebuilt. The screen keeps the rung for its next stream on the stream's path: its next report carries it as `health.rate` ([Hub and realtime channel](../architecture/2026-10-07-hub-and-realtime-channel.md)), and its own new streams of the item, a seek's or a recovery's, ask for it. Another 90 seconds over the rung after it keeps that one instead; a step down drops what was kept. From the top rung, or at the path's ceiling, the kept step is `maxVideoKbps: null`, no cap. The `quality_rate_kept` event records it.
- **Faster than normal speed** ([two engines](../architecture/2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md#playback-speed)), the stream needs that much more throughput: at 2, a step down needs throughput below twice the bitrate × 1.15 and takes half as much for the new cap, and a step up needs twice the rung's bitrate × 1.6. A slower speed asks no less than normal, since the owner can go back to normal at any moment.
- Live streams do not adapt.
- **The owner's choice.** A screen may let the owner pick a rung, or the most the path carries, as the iPhone app's Quality menu does: the stream is rebuilt at it, as a step down is, and the screen neither steps down nor up until the owner goes back to Auto. A stall then stays a stall, which the owner chose over a softer picture. The cap travels as the device's own `maxVideoKbps`, so the Hub keeps it for the path as it keeps any.
- **The browser's own switching.** The cap is the ceiling for the `<video>` engine too: hls.js and native HLS switch by themselves among the variants of a multi-variant playlist that the node kept under the cap, and Yolo's step down adapts a stream with one variant. Where hls.js switches variants itself, the variant is its choice: Yolo asks for no new stream of it, after a stall either, and only keeps a step up for the next stream.
- **Only on a current measurement, and not at the end.** When no download has finished for 30 seconds and none is in flight, the buffer drains because nothing is being fetched, which no bitrate fixes, so the cap moves neither down nor up. Thirty seconds is more than two of the longest segments (10 seconds of audio) and the prefetcher's 12-second inactivity timeout. The canvas engine's prefetcher reports the age of its newest sample; the `<video>` engine reports none and keeps the rules above. With less than 20 seconds of the item left, or the rest of it already buffered, quality is not rebuilt at all: a new stream waits for Yolo and the source and plays only once 3 seconds of it are buffered, which costs more of the item than it can save. On 2026-10-16 a car stepped down on an estimate 3.5 minutes old, 11 seconds before the end, after its read-ahead had stopped ([a refused read-ahead](../bug-fix/2026-10-16-refused-read-ahead.md)).

On the service side:

- The Hub keeps one rate per device and media path (`lan`, `wan`, `relay`) in its Durable Object storage (`rates:<deviceId>`), without expiry: the `maxVideoKbps` of the device's own requests on that path (`null` too), and the `health.rate` its reports carry for a step up it kept. A play on the device that names no `maxVideoKbps` starts at the rate kept for the path it takes: the screen's own play, an agent's, the queue's next item, a transfer, a resume. A rate kept on one path never applies to another, and a page names its cap only on the path it learned it on. Without a kept rate, a stream starts at the path's start: the LAN at its ceiling (no cap), remote direct play and the relay at 1500 kbps, which played a car's drive on the relay over LTE without a stall. An agent cannot set the rate for a device.
- The node takes the lower of the device's cap and its own ceiling for the stream's [media path](../architecture/2026-10-13-media-off-the-worker.md), at least 200 kbps, and passes it to the source's `prepare` as `limits.maxVideoKbps`; the stream descriptor's `limits: { maxVideoKbps, ceilingKbps, capped }` tells the device which cap applied. When every HLS variant exceeds the cap, the smallest stays ([media pipeline](../architecture/2026-10-11-node-media-pipeline.md)). The ceilings are node settings:

| Path | Setting | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| [LAN direct play](2026-10-12-lan-direct-play.md) | `LAN_MAX_VIDEO_KBPS` (at least 500) | 200,000 kbps | The home network carries any original, a disc remux included |
| Remote direct play | `WAN_MAX_VIDEO_KBPS` (at least 500) | 20,000 kbps | The home's upload carries it to one screen: enough for 1440p and 2160p web video and a 2160p Plex transcode |
| The relay through Cloudflare | `STREAM_MAX_VIDEO_BITRATE_KBPS` (500 to 20,000) | 4800 kbps | Conservative, because the relay is the fallback and every byte crosses Cloudflare twice |

- The yt-dlp plugin picks formats under the cap, or the smallest when none fits. Under 8 Mbps the Plex plugin transcodes at the highest of its checked rungs within the cap: 720p at its configured bitrate (4000 to 4800 kbps), else 1500 kbps at 854×480, which Plex decodes at 852×480. A rung is checked when its decoded picture was seen on the owner's Plex server, so a higher rate never brings a smaller picture: asked for 720p at 2200 kbps, Plex made 720×404. Under 1500 it transcodes at 700 kbps and asks for 640×360: Plex never decodes a picture larger than the size asked, so that rung cannot break the order, and a link under about 1.7 Mbps steps down instead of stalling. A cap under 700 still gets 700, and the device then asks for nothing lower on that item. Where the cap carries more than 720p, a `<video>` or native device's picture is its own size ([capability negotiation](../architecture/2026-10-07-capability-negotiation.md)). The device's `maxHeight` still applies on top: a cap only ever lowers quality.
- A source may start a stream below the cap when its upstream measured too slow for the formats it selected, as the yt-dlp plugin does for a slow Bilibili mirror; the stream then reports that lower cap with `capped: true`, so the device steps up from it by the rule above, and the next stream at the kept rate keeps the formats it asked for ([media pipeline](../architecture/2026-10-11-node-media-pipeline.md)). The page's own new streams of the item ask for that cap, which the Hub then keeps for the path.

The player shows no technical numbers while it waits, only the spinner ([device telemetry](2026-10-11-device-telemetry.md)). It keeps the last picture under the spinner until the new stream shows one, and leaves the controls as they were, as every wait does ([in-car design](../architecture/2026-07-13-in-car-design-principles.md)); unlike a seek or a track change, which the owner asked for, a quality change does not show the progress bar's seek indicator either. The `quality_change_requested`, `quality_rate_kept`, `quality_change_failed`, `bandwidth_insufficient`, and `network_unstable` events record each decision ([device telemetry](2026-10-11-device-telemetry.md)).

## Alternatives considered

**Keep the selected quality fixed**, as the original Tesla-only player did: its in-car checks required that quality never change automatically, and Plex's fixed 720p transcode keeps the player's 210-second buffer intact. A weak connection then stalls with no way out short of the owner picking another stream.

**Switch variants inside one stream, as native HLS players do.** The canvas engine cannot: it plays one rendition per stream, so every quality change is a new stream. The `<video>` engine does, among the variants under the cap, but transcodes and joined split files have one variant, so the cap still adapts them.

**Let agents set the cap.** The cap reflects the device's own connection, which only the device measures.

**Rebuild the stream to step up too**, with a 2-minute wait after a step down and a wait that doubled after an up then a down. A step up came only with a full buffer, so the step that discarded the most was the one taken when nothing was wrong: on 2026-10-21 a car on the relay threw away 216 buffered seconds to go from 1500 to 2200 kbps, and Plex then made a smaller picture than before. The waits only limited the cost of that rebuild.

**One remembered cap for 30 minutes on every path.** A drive the next morning started at the relay's ceiling and spent its first minute stalling down to a rate the car already knew, and a cap learned on the relay applied on the LAN.

**More Plex rungs, such as 2200, 1000, 700, and 400 kbps.** Plex picks the picture for a bitrate itself, and an unchecked rung can decode smaller than the rung below it. 3000 kbps at 720p and 700 kbps at 360p join once checked.

## Consequences

Weak connections degrade to a lower bitrate instead of stalling, and a step up costs nothing: the buffer stays and playback never pauses for it. The higher rate waits for the next stream, though, so a long film started low stays low until the screen's next stream (the next item, a seek outside the buffer, a recovery). A screen starts where it last played on that path, also the next day, so a car on the relay starts at a rate that worked rather than at the relay's ceiling; on a day the link is better, it starts lower than it could. Without history the relay and remote direct play start at 1500 kbps, which is 480p for Plex. A step down still rebuilds and starts from an empty buffer. Plex has no rung under 1500 kbps, so a link that carries less stalls at 1500 rather than dropping to 360p.
