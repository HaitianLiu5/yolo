# Agent Note: Danmaku over the picture

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-10-13-danmaku.zh.md)

## Problem

Bilibili videos come with danmaku: viewers' comments, each timed to a moment of the video, that scroll across the picture or stay at its top or bottom. They are much of why people watch there, and Yolo played the video without them. Showing them touches every layer: Bilibili is one provider among plugins that core code must not name; the page asks no host but Yolo's own; text over the picture is the player's job, not the source's; and in the car the canvas engine presents video frames on the page's main thread, where any long task shows as a dropped frame, while comments move over the picture all the time.

## Decision

### Comments are an optional source capability

A source plugin may implement `comments(source, { start, end })` ([source plugin API](../architecture/2026-10-11-source-plugin-api.md)). It answers `null` for an item without comments, or `{ comments, start?, end? }`: comments `{ t, text, mode?, color?, size?, weight? }` (seconds; `scroll`, `top`, or `bottom`; `#rrggbb`; a size relative to the normal one; the provider's rank of the comment, 0 to 10, higher for those its viewers want to see) and the span they cover completely, at least from `start`, such as one segment the provider serves or the whole item (`end: null`). `normalizeComments` in [`service/core/src/danmaku.js`](../../../../service/core/src/danmaku.js) drops comments without a time or text, cuts text to 100 characters, and keeps at most 20,000 per answer. No core, control, or node code names a provider.

The node keeps the answers ([`service/node/src/comments.js`](../../../../service/node/src/comments.js)): it asks the source one 6-minute window at a time, once at a time per window, records which windows an answer covers, and serves any range from what it holds. A failed window is asked again only after a minute. At most 200,000 comments over at most 16 items are kept, the least recently used item first out. When a session of an item whose source can have comments opens, the node reads the window where playback starts while the screen loads. The same store answers two places:

- **The playing screen** reads `/m/{token}/comments?start=&end=` from its media session ([media pipeline](../architecture/2026-10-11-node-media-pipeline.md)), whose stream descriptor names that path (`comments`) when the source can have comments and the stream is not live. It comes from the stream's own origin, the node's LAN or remote listener or Yolo's relay, so the page asks no other host, and comments, like media, stay off the Worker unless the owner has the relay on.
- **Agents** read through the control plane's `nodeFetch()` to `/internal/v1/comments?source=&start=&end=` ([node's internal API](../architecture/2026-10-07-node-internal-api.md)), which answers `available: false, supported: false` for a source without `comments()`.

### Bilibili in the yt-dlp plugin

[`plugins/source-ytdlp/src/danmaku.js`](../../../../plugins/source-ytdlp/src/danmaku.js) gives comments for Bilibili pages (`bilibili.com`, `b23.tv`) and `null` at once for every other site, without running yt-dlp. yt-dlp names an item's comment XML, `https://comment.bilibili.com/<cid>.xml`, as its `danmaku` subtitle only when subtitles are asked for, so the plugin runs yt-dlp once per page with `--write-subs` (`-j` writes nothing), the same run that lists Bilibili's subtitles ([source plugin API](../architecture/2026-10-11-source-plugin-api.md)), and keeps the answer for 32 pages.

The XML comes first: one request for the whole item, which Bilibili caps at its newest `maxlimit` comments. Measured anonymously on 2026-10-13, it held 2 to 3 times what the segmented protobuf API that Bilibili's web player reads gave for the same videos: 7,200 against 2,827 for a 44-minute video, 3,600 against 1,380 for a 23-minute one, and 600 against 283 for a 2-minute one. When the XML fails and its URL is Bilibili's own, the plugin reads the 6-minute segment of `api.bilibili.com/x/v2/dm/web/seg.so` that holds `start`; a segment past the end answers 304 and has none. Modes 1 to 3 and 6 scroll, 4 is bottom and 5 top; scripted comments (modes 7, 8, and 9, and the special pool) are dropped. Each comment keeps Bilibili's `weight`, the rank from 1 to 10 its "smart blocking" (智能云屏蔽) hides comments by: the XML's ninth field, a segment's field 9.

Requests carry a browser's User-Agent and Bilibili's Referer, as the plugin's media requests do, and the cookies of the configured cookies file (`cookiesFile` or `YTDLP_COOKIES_FILE`) for the host. Cookies from a browser profile (`cookiesFromBrowser`) reach only yt-dlp, so with them comments are fetched anonymously. An answer is at most 8 MiB and waits at most 15 seconds.

### Drawing them

How comments look and move follows Bilibili's web player with its default settings, read from its code on 2026-10-14 (its settings defaults in `core.<hash>.js` and its renderer in `npd.491.<hash>.js` under `s1.hdslb.com/bfs/static/player/main/`), not an open-source danmaku engine; Alternatives considered says why. [`danmaku-layout.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/danmaku-layout.js) places comments as a function of media time:

- **Size.** A normal comment's text is 25 CSS pixels whatever the picture's size, in lanes 30 pixels apart, as Bilibili's web player draws it: the owner's screenshots of one video on Bilibili, in a small window and full screen on the same screen, show the same size. Yolo first followed the curve in Bilibili's code for "scale with the screen" (弹幕随屏幕缩放), which reached 42 px over a full-screen 1080 px picture, nearly the size of the subtitles. The text size setting scales it by 0.82 or 1.2, and a comment's own size (Bilibili's 18, 25, or 36) scales it again. The text is bold, weight 700, as Bilibili's is; the build serves Noto Sans SC and Noto Sans Latin at 700 for it. The lanes start 6 CSS pixels below the picture's top, where Bilibili's first row of Chinese text begins; with a lane flush at the top, Yolo's first row touched the edge.
- **Pace.** A scrolling comment crosses the picture in a time set by the picture's width, Bilibili's table: 4.5 seconds below 558 CSS pixels, 7.1 up to 888, 8.1 up to 1082, 9.1 up to 1246, 10.1 up to 1452, 11.1 up to 1744, 13.1 up to 2314, 14.1 up to 2560, then 16.1 and one more every 500 pixels; so a longer comment moves faster. A top or bottom comment stays 4.5 seconds, centred.
- **Lanes.** The three modes share one set of lanes, and no two comments on screen overlap: a comment takes the first lane, from the top or, for a bottom comment, from the bottom, where it keeps a gap (half a lane, at least 12 CSS pixels) from every comment already there for as long as both are on screen. Between two scrolling comments that is Bilibili's rule, and weizhenye/danmaku's: the earlier one has fully entered the picture, plus the gap, and the later one, faster when it is longer, does not catch up with it before the earlier one leaves; here it is exact over the whole time both show. A top or bottom comment so takes a lane where no scrolling comment crosses its place while it shows, and covers none. A comment larger than the normal size takes two lanes. A lane is as tall as the caption font's ink at the text size (a CJK ideograph, Latin capitals, and descenders, measured once in the text worker), an outline of 1 CSS pixel on each side, and 1 pixel of gap, Bilibili's spacing, rounded up to whole device pixels at the device pixel ratio times the car's CSS zoom; a comment's bitmap is exactly its lanes' height, so none draws into another lane.
- **Density.** At `normal` density, comments whose `weight` is below 3 are hidden, as Bilibili's smart blocking does at its default level (`aiLevel: 3`), and no more scrolling comments enter in a second than the area has lanes, Bilibili's budget (`getRollDanmakuMaxCountPerSecond`). Bilibili admits that budget in one batch, so a burst enters in one frame and reads as a column of comments, one per lane. Here comments enter one at a time, the budget spread over the second: each a lane's share of a second (one second over the lanes) after the one before on average, varied from half to one and a half of that per comment, never two in one frame, and, while another lane fits, not in the lane above or below the comment that entered less than a second before, so a burst into empty lanes interleaves instead of stacking into a staircase. A comment waits up to 1 second for its turn and a lane at `low` and `normal` density and 2 seconds at `high`, and is dropped after that, as Bilibili drops what does not fit. `low` keeps about a third, the same third every time; `high` keeps the low-ranked comments too.
- **Time.** A paused picture shows the same comments in the same places, and a faster playback rate moves them faster. A load or a seek starts the screen empty: comments from before that moment never show, and those after it enter at the edge, as after a seek in DPlayer and CommentCoreLibrary. A jump of more than a second forward or a quarter-second back is a seek, and so is a first frame more than a second after the stream's start. New comments continue the layout from the last moment laid out; a new size or density lays the same moment out again as it played since the last load or seek, from at most 16 seconds before. At most 200 comments are on screen at once.

[`danmaku.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/danmaku.js) draws them on one `<canvas>` over the picture's own rectangle in either engine, above the picture and below the subtitles and the controls. It reads the playing engine's position each animation frame, and with the canvas engine, which counts the frames it draws (`presentedFrames`), it draws only when the picture changes, so the page still updates at the video's frame rate. The animation frame runs only while comments are shown, the page is visible, and the position moves; a pause draws one frame and stops it. Sizes are read when the window, the picture, the display size, or a setting changes, never per frame; the backing store follows the CSS zoom of the car's display size and the device pixel ratio, at most 2 device pixels per CSS pixel. The font is the page's caption fonts at the size above, with Bilibili's default outline (描边类型 重墨): a dark edge about 1 CSS pixel wide around the glyphs, white for black text; opacity is the layer's CSS opacity.

Text never touches the main thread's frames: a worker ([`danmaku-worker.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/danmaku-worker.js)) measures every comment of a window when it arrives, after loading the parts of the page's caption fonts the comments need (it adds them from the page's `@font-face` rules), and draws each comment once, with its outline, into an ImageBitmap, the first time it is on screen. A comment shows from the next frame after its bitmap arrives; a scrolling comment then is a few pixels from the edge. A frame is one clear and one `drawImage` per comment, and at most 300 bitmaps and 6 million pixels of them are kept. A browser that cannot draw text in a worker measures and draws on the page ([`danmaku-text.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/danmaku-text.js)). The screen reads the window of comments where it plays, and the next one 30 seconds before it gets there.

### Settings, per screen

Each screen keeps its own settings in the browser ([`danmaku-settings.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/danmaku-settings.js)), checked against [`service/core/src/danmaku.js`](../../../../service/core/src/danmaku.js):

| Setting | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| On or off | | On |
| Density | `low` (about a third, the same third every time), `normal` (those that fit without overlapping, entering one at a time, less those ranked below 3), `high` (also the low-ranked ones, waiting up to 2 seconds for a lane; none overlap) | `normal` |
| Area | `full`, `half`, `quarter` of the picture, from its top | `quarter` |
| Opacity | 0.2 to 1; the sheet offers 40, 60, 80, and 100% | 0.8 |
| Text size | `small`, `normal`, `large` | `normal` |

The defaults are the same in the car and on the web, and are Bilibili's web player's own (`dmSetting` in its code): on, 80% opacity (`opacity: 0.8`), its normal density with smart blocking at level 3 (`dmdensity: 1`, `aiLevel: 3`), and text at 100% (`fontsize: 1`) at its moderate speed (`speedplus: 1`). Comments show only for an item that has them, and its viewers expect them, as Bilibili shows them by default; the car's media computer (an AMD Ryzen running Chrome 148) has the headroom, and what danmaku cost the main thread is measured below. The area is the one exception: Bilibili's web player ships at half the picture (`dmarea: 50`) but carries each account's own choice, and on BV1soWF6qEjF the owner's Bilibili showed comments in the top quarter, about five rows, where Yolo's full area covered the picture with about seventeen. The top quarter keeps the car's picture clear, holds 4 lanes in the car and 6 in a 1440 by 900 window, and with the density above shows what Bilibili's moderate density looks like there. The full area keeps the bottom fifth of the picture free while subtitles show, so comments never cover them.

The browser keeps only the settings chosen on that screen, with `version: 2`, so a screen follows the defaults for what it never chose, and a change of default reaches it. Yolo 0.6.0 kept every setting once one changed; from such a record, a value other than 0.6.0's default (`full` area) counts as a choice, and one equal to it gives way to the current default.

A button in the player's controls turns danmaku on and off; it shows only while the item has comments. Settings > This screen has the switch and the four choices as 48-pixel steps. An agent changes them with `set_danmaku`: the Hub sends the screen a `danmaku` message ([Hub and realtime channel](../architecture/2026-10-07-hub-and-realtime-channel.md)), and the screen applies and keeps the change. Until the screen acknowledges it, the Hub keeps the change and sends it again after the screen's next welcome, so a connection that died without anyone noticing does not lose it; a setting the owner changed on the screen meanwhile stays as the owner set it, unless the page was loaded again since.

### What agents read

`get_comments` reads the comments around a moment, by default the position of the item playing, compactly: a window of 30 seconds (at most 120), `{ t, text }` in time order, the same text said several times as one entry with a count `n`, and past 60 entries (at most 200) the most repeated and those nearest the moment ([remote MCP server](2026-10-07-remote-mcp-server.md)).

### Telemetry

The player's `health_heartbeat` ([device telemetry](2026-10-11-device-telemetry.md)) carries `danmaku`, what the layer did since the last heartbeat: `enabled`, `available`, `comments` (held), `frames` drawn, `avgDrawMs` and `maxDrawMs` (the main thread's time to lay out and draw one frame), `slowFrames` (draws over 5 ms), `bitmapsDrawn`, `maxOnScreen`, and `bitmaps` (kept).

### Measured cost

Desktop Chrome 155 on an Apple-silicon Mac, in a visible window at the car's size (773 by 601 CSS pixels at device pixel ratio 1.53, the car profile, the canvas engine, the default display size), playing 720p30 H.264 HLS with real Bilibili comments retimed into a fixture, in steady state after the HLS prefetcher's first burst. Each row is 8 pairs of 6-second windows, danmaku off then on; CPU is summed over Chrome's processes and is the median of the pairs' differences, because the Mac's scheduling between its core types moves the baseline by up to 10 points between windows.

| Fixture | On screen at once | Chrome's CPU, off → on | Median difference (GPU process, renderer) | Video frames dropped, off / on | Long tasks, off / on | Danmaku's main-thread time per drawn frame: average, longest (medians of the windows) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical: 3 comments a second | up to 25 | 30.5% → 33.5% | +3.0 points (+2.1, +1.3) | 0 of 1,466 / 0 of 1,466 | 0 / 0 | 0.22 ms, 0.64 ms |
| Dense: 12 comments a second | up to 92 | 32.8% → 44.0% | +12.0 points (+7.3, +4.8) | 15 of 1,435 / 12 of 1,443 | 3 / 3 | 0.55 ms, 1.37 ms |

Draws over 5 ms were 1 of about 1,450 at the typical rate and 4 at the dense one, and there were as many long tasks with danmaku off as on: the main thread presents the video as smoothly with danmaku as without. The CPU they cost is mostly the GPU process's and the compositor's, and grows with the comments on screen.

These were measured with 0.6.0's layout over the full picture. The layout tuned on 2026-10-14 shows fewer comments at once by default, and its own work stays small: timed in Node on the real comments of BV1soWF6qEjF (8,593 over 64 minutes) through their densest two minutes, one update took at most 0.4 ms with the default top quarter and 0.2 ms over the full picture at `high` density, and laying a moment out again at most 0.6 ms. The CPU table above was not measured again.

## Alternatives considered

**The segmented protobuf API first.** It is what Bilibili's web player reads, by 6-minute segments, but anonymously it gave 2 to 3 times fewer comments than the XML for the same videos, and one request per segment instead of one per item.

**DOM elements moved by CSS animations.** The compositor would move them without the main thread, but each comment would be an element to create, style, and lay out on the main thread, and pausing, seeking, and changing the rate would mean adjusting every running animation against the engine's clock, which the canvas engine keeps on the main thread anyway.

**Drawing text on the main thread.** The first version drew each comment's bitmap there, a few per frame: measured at car size, one bitmap took up to 15 to 25 ms, for an emoji or a part of a font used for the first time, which is a long frame. Measuring every comment's text when the layout placed it cost up to 23 ms after a seek.

**Drawing at the display's rate.** The page would update twice per frame of a 30-frame video; drawing when the canvas engine draws keeps the page's updates where they were.

**Serving comments through `/v1` with the device credential.** It works through the Worker on every path, but comments then always cross Cloudflare, and the media session already reaches the screen from the node by the screen's own path.

**Off by default in the car.** The car's media computer is a Ryzen, and the measured cost on the main thread is a fraction of a millisecond per frame; no reason other than cost argued for it.

**Caching in the plugin.** Every source would have to bound its own cache; the node's store bounds them all, and a source only answers spans.

**An open-source danmaku engine instead of the layout.** Measured against Bilibili's behaviour on 2026-10-14: CommentCoreLibrary (MIT) never drops a comment but lays one that fits nowhere over the others in another pool, and its catch-up test is a heuristic; weizhenye/danmaku (MIT) has the exact fully-entered and catch-up test this layout uses, but wraps a comment that fits nowhere back over the others, and its default pace is a fixed speed of 144 px/s, so its crossing time grows with the width; ArtPlayer's danmuku plugin (MIT) queues a comment that fits nowhere with no limit, so it can appear long after its time, and feeds its worker from DOM measurements; DPlayer (MIT) drops what fits nowhere, but lays out with DOM elements and CSS animations. None draws in a worker on the engine's clock while keeping comments from ever overlapping, so the layout stays Yolo's, with Bilibili's sizes, pace, and density, and the seek behaviour of DPlayer and CommentCoreLibrary. No code of theirs is copied.

**A burst entering in one frame, as Bilibili does.** A dozen comments at the same moment, as an opening has, stack into a column one lane tall each; taken one at a time, they still all fit in the second Bilibili allows them.

**Restoring comments from before a seek, as Bilibili's player does.** It reads comments from the picture's crossing time before the new moment and shows them mid-way; with a layout that has to be measured and drawn, a screenful then pops in at once. The screen starts empty instead, and comments enter at the edge.

**Merging duplicate comments.** Bilibili's web player has no such setting, its app's "block repeated comments" (屏蔽重复弹幕) is off by default, and its Android engine, DanmakuFlameMaster, merges none by default, so Yolo merges none; agents get repeated text merged with a count.

**The full picture, or Bilibili's web half, by default.** Yolo 0.6.0 used the full picture: about seventeen lanes of text, larger than Bilibili's over the same picture, covered it. Half the picture is Bilibili's web default, but at the opening of a busy video it fills ten lanes, where the owner's Bilibili showed five.

## Consequences

Bilibili videos play with their danmaku in the car and on the web, an agent can say what viewers say at a moment, and another source gives comments by implementing one method. The layer costs the GPU process and the compositor the most, because it adds a picture-sized layer that changes with every video frame; its main-thread work is a fraction of a millisecond per frame. A comment appears a frame after its bitmap is drawn, and a window's comments appear after their widths are measured. The XML caps comments at Bilibili's `maxlimit`, the newest ones; scripted comments are not drawn; comments with a browser profile's cookies are anonymous. After a load or a seek the screen starts empty, so comments sent just before that moment are not seen; at `normal` density a fifth of Bilibili's comments, those it ranks lowest, are hidden, and comments wait up to a second to enter one at a time. Settings stay on each screen, so a new screen starts from the defaults, and an agent cannot read them back.

## Testing

[`danmaku-layout.test.js`](../../../../clients/player/test/unit/danmaku-layout.test.js) pins Bilibili's size and pace curves, lanes, timing through pause, seek, and rate, that a load or a seek shows nothing from before it, density and the hidden low-ranked comments, and the on-screen bound; it checks over a dense fixture that no two scheduled comments ever overlap, exactly over each pair's time on screen, through a pause, seeks, and a resize, that no two comments enter within one frame even when a dozen share a moment, and that with the default settings comments stay in the top quarter of the car's picture and of a computer's; [`danmaku-settings.test.js`](../../../../clients/player/test/unit/danmaku-settings.test.js) checks that only choices are kept and that 0.6.0's records keep theirs; [`danmaku.test.js`](../../../../plugins/source-ytdlp/test/danmaku.test.js) parses the XML fixture and a protobuf segment, with their ranks, cookies, and the fallback; [`comments.test.js`](../../../../service/node/test/comments.test.js) the node's windows, failures, and bound; [`mcp.test.js`](../../../../service/control/test/unit/mcp.test.js) the two tools. [`test/e2e/danmaku.test.js`](../../../../test/e2e/danmaku.test.js) plays a Bilibili page through a fake yt-dlp and a fixture XML on the car's canvas engine: comments appear at their times through a pause and seeks, none from before a seek, inside the picture, an agent reads them and turns them off, and the page requests no host but Yolo's.
