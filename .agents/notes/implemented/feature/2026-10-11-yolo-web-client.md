# Agent Note: Yolo Web, a browser client with settings

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-10-11-yolo-web-client.zh.md)

## Problem

The player in the car was Yolo's only screen. On a computer or phone, the owner could not browse the library, play in the browser, or use the browser as a remote for the car. There was no settings page either: revoking an agent meant deleting its records from `OAUTH_KV` by hand, passkeys and devices could be managed only through `/v1` and MCP, and the car's display size sat as a row of percentage chips at the bottom of the home screen, where on a Mac it showed meaningless steps (100%, 77%, 68%, …).

## Decision

### One app with two modes

The car's screen and Yolo Web are one app, the player ([`clients/player`](../../../../clients/player)), served at the site root as one bundle whose [`player.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/player.js) drives two playback engines ([two engines](../architecture/2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md)). [`mode.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/mode.js) chooses a mode and sets it as `<html data-mode>`:

| | `car` | `web` |
|---|---|---|
| When | The owner's device type is "car", or, until Yolo has said the type, the [Tesla fingerprint](2026-10-07-device-code-pairing.md) matches | Every other type: a computer's or phone's browser |
| Display size | Offered in settings ([display size](2026-10-11-display-size.md)) | None: the browser's own zoom |
| Engine | The canvas engine | The canvas engine on a page the Tesla fingerprint recognizes; otherwise the browser's `<video>` element, or the canvas engine when only that runs |
| Capabilities in `hello` | `CAR_CANVAS_CAPABILITIES` | `CAR_CANVAS_CAPABILITIES` on a page the Tesla fingerprint recognizes; otherwise `WEB_NATIVE_CAPABILITIES` with the browser's codecs and a `maxHeight` of 2160, 1440, 1080, or 720 by display (`displayMaxHeight`), `WEB_CANVAS_CAPABILITIES` with the canvas engine, or `REMOTE_CAPABILITIES` when the browser can play with neither ([capability negotiation](../architecture/2026-10-07-capability-negotiation.md)) |
| A tap on an item | Plays on this screen | Plays where playback is now (below) |
| Pairing | The QR code first | The owner's passkey first, the code as the way from another device |

The owner's type (chosen when pairing, or later in settings) wins over the fingerprint for the mode (the layout and the display size) and is remembered for the next visit; a change applies to the layout at once. The type does not decide the engine of a page the Tesla fingerprint recognizes: such a page always plays with the canvas engine and announces `CAR_CANVAS_CAPABILITIES`, so the type cannot correct a false match there ([two engines](../architecture/2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md)). On any other page a change of type applies to the engine from the next stream and to the capabilities from the next connection. COOP/COEP on every page of the app and the autoplay rule (muted with "Tap for sound" when the browser blocks sound) hold in both modes; canvas rendering is the car's.

### Layout across window sizes

- **The car**, about 773×601 CSS pixels at a pixel ratio of 1.53, lays the page out 946 pixels wide at the default display size. A slim header (the Yolo mark, Ask, and Ask's microphone on the left, where the driver reaches them, then search and settings) sits above the rows; settings open as a sheet over the right two thirds of the window (`min(620px, 66cqw)`). The browser window ends above the car's app dock, so nothing is kept clear of it ([design principles](../architecture/2026-07-13-in-car-design-principles.md)).
- **A computer** shows the same rows with wider gutters, search centred and at most 560 pixels wide, settings as a sheet from the right edge (`clamp(380px, 42cqw, 560px)`), and the now-playing bar floating at most 960 pixels wide.
- **A phone** (640 pixels or narrower) shows the mark without its word, settings over the whole window, the device picker as a bottom sheet, and the now-playing bar with the device's name only; rows put their actions under their text instead of squeezing it.

### Where a tap plays

[`play-target.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/play-target.js) decides. A tap plays on the screen it was made on whenever the browser can play, in both modes: every screen has its own session, so a tap here never stops what plays on another screen, and controlling another screen is an explicit act in the now-playing bar ([screen sessions](../architecture/2026-10-31-screen-sessions.md)). A browser that cannot play sends the tap to the only other online screen that can; with several, the owner picks one. When someone else's session is in use there, the Hub refuses with `screen_busy` and the screen asks before it plays there with `replace: true`.

### The remote

While a session is in use on another screen, or this screen's own was left with Back or continued elsewhere, the home screen shows a now-playing bar ([`remote.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/remote.js)). It controls one session at a time: this screen's own left or continued one first, else the newest other session in use, or the one the owner picked. It shows the item, "Playing on" the screen (or "Continued on" the screen that took this one's), the session's profile when it is not the screen's default, a progress line extrapolated from the Hub's `position` and `positionAt`, back 10 seconds, play/pause, and next, each sent with the session's `deviceId`. Its sheet lists every session, each with **Continue here** when this browser can play, then **Move to** the other screens that play, which moves the controlled session with `PUT /v1/playback/device` `{ deviceId, from }`; a `screen_busy` refusal asks "Play there instead?" and the confirm sends `replace: true`. Taking a continued session back is one tap on the bar. Every command goes over `/v1` with this device's credential; the bar only reads the Hub's `state`, `queue`, and `devices` broadcasts and never answers them ([Hub](../architecture/2026-10-07-hub-and-realtime-channel.md)).

At the top of home, **Continue from another screen** lists up to three other screens' sessions of the owner's profile or this screen's default one, with an item not finished, newest first, offline screens' included, such as a parked car's; one tap continues the session here with its whole context, and the other screen pauses. The screen asks `POST /v1/playback/warm` for the first card's item at its position ahead of its history items, so the tap starts as fast as a warmed history item. A remote has no card. A player whose session another screen continued leaves to the home screen, and a returning page does not resume it ([`resume-decision.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/resume-decision.js)).

This screen's session (`hub`), every other screen's (`sessions`, by device id), this screen's queue, and the playlists, from a broadcast, a welcome, a home load, or the answer to this screen's own change, land in one place ([`store.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/store.js)), which redraws each view that shows them (the queue and playlists rows, the bar, the card, the player's previous and next), decides whether previous and next have anywhere to go, and says which sessions are in use (`inUse()`) and which to offer for continuing (`continueCandidates()`).

### Search

The header's search field queries `/v1/library/search` as the owner types (after 300 ms, from two characters); a pasted http(s) link offers **Play link** and **Add to queue**, and a source plugin claims it when it plays.

### Settings

Settings are a modal sheet at `/settings` ([`settings.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/settings.js)), opened from the gear in the home header, in three groups:

- **This screen**: under the heading, the name and type Yolo has for this device; then the display size (car only), **Danmaku** with, while it is on, its density, area, opacity, and text size ([danmaku](2026-10-13-danmaku.md)), **Home network**, the switch for playing over it, with what this screen's last probe found ([LAN direct play](2026-10-12-lan-direct-play.md)), **Media path**, the path this screen's next stream takes, with why the direct ones did not answer when it is the relay or none ([media off the Worker](../architecture/2026-10-13-media-off-the-worker.md)), **Ask with**, the agent this screen's Ask asks, **Microphone** with its test ([screens ask the owner's own agent](2026-10-18-agent-from-the-screen.md)), and last **Forget this screen** (`DELETE /v1/devices/self`), which unpairs it. The device credential is enough for these.
- **Your Yolo**: devices (each with its type, whether it is online, the path it played by last, and who watches there when it is not the owner; one Edit opens the name, the type as a segmented choice, **Who watches here**, the screen's default profile, and Remove in place), **Who watches**, the profiles (the owner's first, then each other one with its name to rename, its screens, and Remove, which deletes its history; a field adds one; [screen sessions](../architecture/2026-10-31-screen-sessions.md)), connected agents (one row per OAuth client, revoke) and **Reset image links**, which makes every cover link agents were given stop working ([cover images](2026-10-07-remote-mcp-server.md#cover-images)), the voice languages (the languages the owner speaks, first first, with Up, Remove, and a button for each language to add; [voice input](2026-10-18-agent-from-the-screen.md#voice-input)), share links ([share links](2026-10-20-share-links.md)), passkeys (list, add another, remove all but the last), the media paths (the home network's state and the node's address, with a check now; the remote direct play switch; the relay's mode), and signing out. They use the [owner routes](../architecture/2026-10-07-v1-http-api.md#owner-routes) behind the owner's passkey session, never the device credential. Signing in runs WebAuthn on the page with the existing `/auth/passkey/*` endpoints, so playback is not interrupted; the car, whose browser may not do passkeys, also shows a QR code that opens these settings on a phone.
- **About**: the version and commit, the media node's state and version, and `/diag`.

Each row says one thing: its name, its state in a few words, and a fix only where the owner has one to make; detail an owner reads only while diagnosing (certificate dates, router mapping methods) is left to `yolo doctor`. On and off are switches. A choice of a few sits beside its name and wraps under it on a narrow sheet. The sheet works with touch and with a keyboard: 40 px targets in 48 px rows, since settings are changed parked ([design principles](../architecture/2026-07-13-in-car-design-principles.md)), nothing that needs hover, focus kept inside, Escape and a visible Close (and a tap beside the sheet) to leave, and back closes it. A destructive action asks once more in place ("Remove?") instead of opening another dialog.

### Pairing a browser

A browser pairs with the same device grant as the car ([device code pairing](2026-10-07-device-code-pairing.md)). In web mode the pairing screen leads with the owner's passkey: signed in on this browser (or after signing in there), one press approves the page's own code through `POST /v1/owner/pairing/approve`. It is the same single-use, 10-minute code and the same explicit approval, without a second device; another site cannot make the request (the owner routes' same-origin checks).

### Starting up

A paired screen never waits for its realtime channel before it shows something, and never says "Connecting…". It keeps what it shows in `localStorage`, for the paired device only (`yolo.home.<device id>`, [`home-cache.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/home-cache.js)): the home screen's rows (the queue, playlists, Continue Watching, and the libraries' rows, each at most 200 items, the libraries dropped first when the record would pass 1.5 MB), the Hub's last `state`, and Yolo's devices, written a second after a change in what a start would show (not for a position alone) and when the page is hidden or goes away. Unpairing or forgetting the screen deletes it, and a write it still had pending never brings it back. A record a start cannot draw (a shape the release does not read) is deleted too, and the page starts as with nothing kept, the device's events saying `start_cache_failed`; it connects either way. Before the first paint, the first script of [`index.html`](../../../../clients/player/index.html) looks for the credential and that record and sets `<html data-boot>`; [`main.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/main.js) then shows the first screen while the channel connects:

- **With a record (a warm start):** the home screen as it was, with the now-playing bar from the kept state, still (its line does not run on). When the kept state says this screen would resume an item, as [`resume-decision.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/resume-decision.js) decides for a returning page, it shows that item's loading screen, with its artwork, instead. Yolo's answers then replace the rows in place: a row whose items are the same is not drawn again, so nothing flickers or loads its artwork twice.
- **Without one (a cold start):** the first open in a browser, or after its storage was cleared. The page paints the launch mark ([`launch.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/launch.js)): the Yolo mark comes up dim, one band of light crosses it, and it is lit, under a faint glow, in under a second; with reduced motion it shows lit and still. It has no minimum: the first screen with something to show (the welcome's resume, the home screen, a show, pairing, or a message) ends it with a 360 ms fade. A welcome later than 1.2 seconds after the start brings the home screen with skeletons instead.

Everything shown before the welcome is for looking only. Where a tap plays, what a returning page resumes, the kept player, and missed commands read only the welcome. A tap on an item before it waits for it, then decides where to play ([`welcome-wait.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/welcome-wait.js)): only the latest tap waits, its card held pressed, with the loading ring past the ring's delay; one made meanwhile takes its place, and one whose wait fails is dropped: when the channel stops or the screen pairs again, quietly; when no welcome comes within the 20 seconds a request waits for one, with the home screen's one-line notice "Yolo did not answer in time. Try again." In the car, which always plays on itself, the item's loading screen shows at once, and its request waits for the welcome as every request does ([wake request ordering](../bug-fix/2026-10-11-wake-request-ordering.md)). Such a tap wins over resuming: the first welcome after it, of the page or since it paired again, resumes nothing. The bar's buttons work the same way: the latest press waits, its button dimmed. Settings opened meanwhile know this screen by its credential. The home's data is asked for once for a start and its welcome: the requests leave with the start, and the load that welcome brings uses their answers, but keeps the queue and playlists the welcome sent, which are newer. A welcome that resumes an item or plays a tap drops those answers, so Back from that item asks Yolo again. Every load of the home screen (a start, Back from the player, a reconnect) shows it at once, its rows as they were until new ones replace them; a reconnect's load keeps where it is scrolled. A start on a show's address shows its episodes without waiting for the libraries' rows behind them.

Connecting and reconnecting show nothing over the content. A channel down for 4 seconds ([`connection-indicator.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/connection-indicator.js)) dims the home header's mark, with a small amber dot on its corner, and says so to screen readers; the next welcome clears it. The player and its loading screen show no such mark: playback goes on, and its requests wait for the channel. Only what needs the owner keeps a screen of its own: pairing, which shows the QR plate, the code, and the address as placeholders while it asks Yolo for a code; another window that plays now, whose **Take over here** then waits with the loading ring alone; and errors, each with a way on: **Try again**, and **Home** when it was not the home screen that failed ([`status.js`](../../../../clients/player/src/status.js)). A failure that leaves playback going (subtitles or audio that would not change, an agent canvas press that did not go through) is a one-line notice that goes by itself instead, over the picture or the home screen. When the media node is offline, or Yolo itself does not answer, the home screen keeps the rows it has and shows a band at its top that says so, what still works (the queue, playlists, and history), and **Try again**. The Movies and TV Shows rows each hold every library of their kind: the first 100 items of each come with the home screen, and the next page, a library at a time, as the row nears its end; a page that fails leaves **Try again** at the row's end.

Waiting looks the same everywhere: the launch mark on a cold start, skeletons where items are on their way, with a soft shimmer that crosses a row card after card (still in the car: [design principles](../architecture/2026-07-13-in-car-design-principles.md)), and the item's artwork under the loading ring from a tap until the picture moves ([device telemetry](2026-10-11-device-telemetry.md)). No screen says "Connecting…" or "Loading…".

Measured on the end-to-end stack with 150 ms added to every request, roughly a mobile connection, the median of five reloads in the browser's window and the car's: a reload showed "Connecting…" for 0.7 seconds and the home screen's items after 1.2. A warm start now shows them at 0.56 seconds, just after the first paint, and a cold one at 0.92, with the welcome. A car waking into playback shows the item's loading screen at 0.75 seconds, where it showed it at 1.14, after 0.9 seconds of "Connecting…".

### Testing

[`test/e2e/web-playback.test.js`](../../../../test/e2e/web-playback.test.js) covers playback through the `<video>` engine, Back into the now-playing bar, pairing with the passkey plus playback at 1440×900 and at a phone's 390×844, and owner settings behind the passkey session (a device credential and a cross-site request are refused; rename, revoke an agent, the last passkey kept, sign out). A unit test covers the engine each mode chooses, and the control plane's unit tests the owner routes. [`start-up.test.js`](../../../../clients/player/test/unit/start-up.test.js) covers the kept record (one device's, what `index.html`'s first script finds, its size bound, and that unpairing deletes it for good), the press that waits for the welcome, and the connection mark's delay. The browser's reload after Back, and the car's wake, show the home screen and the bar, or the loading screen, from what the page kept before the welcome, then Yolo's own; a record the page cannot draw starts it cold; Back after the wake shows Yolo's history, not the start's; and the car, removed after a tap and paired again, shows its home screen.

## Alternatives considered

**A device plugin for the web client.** Both phase-one clients are Yolo's own and speak the same protocol, so the realtime channel stays in the core; an interface with a single implementation could not be designed well.

**A separate web app.** Since software 2026.26 the car's browser presents itself as desktop Chrome, so the server cannot route a car and a computer to different apps; two apps would also duplicate the shell, pairing, and realtime code and drift apart. One app chooses its mode on the device instead.

**Cookie authentication on `/v1`, so a signed-in browser needs no pairing.** It would expose every `/v1` route to cross-site requests and break the one-token model of the [OAuth server](../architecture/2026-10-07-oauth-authorization-server.md). Every browser is a paired device instead; the owner's session serves only owner settings and approving the browser's own code.

**Owner settings on the server-rendered `/account` page.** Settings have to work in the car as a touch sheet over the home screen without leaving playback, and they show what only the client knows (this device, its display size). The server-rendered pages keep the flows that must work before a device is paired: sign-in, approving a pairing, and an agent's consent ([owner web pages](../architecture/2026-10-12-owner-web-pages.md)).

**A tap plays where playback is now**, as Spotify Connect does. It keeps a phone used as a remote from taking playback away from the car, but with a session per screen it plays the owner's tap on the TV the family watches. Controlling another screen is the bar's explicit act instead.

**A splash screen until the welcome.** It hides the wait instead of removing it: the ticket, the socket, the hello, and the welcome take three or four round trips before anything could show, 0.7 seconds of "Connecting…" at 150 ms each. What the screen kept shows at once, and the welcome only confirms or replaces it.

**Treating the kept state as Yolo's.** A tap would play where the kept state said, possibly on a device that has stopped since, and a returning page would resume, or apply missed commands, from a state hours old. The kept state only shows; the welcome decides.

## Consequences

The owner can browse, search, play on this screen, and control or continue any other screen's session from a computer or phone, manage devices, agents, and passkeys without touching `OAUTH_KV`, and pair a browser with a passkey alone. The car plays on itself, keeps its canvas rendering, and finds its display size in settings. Safari on iPhone and iPad, which cannot run the canvas engine, plays through its own `<video>` element; only a browser that can play with neither engine is a remote. The mode follows the owner's type for the device, so a car paired as a browser loses its display size until its type is set to car in settings; it still plays with the canvas engine ([two engines](../architecture/2026-10-12-two-playback-engines.md)). Sources and plugins are not managed in settings yet; they stay in `yolo.config.json` on the node. A start shows what the screen kept, which can be a little behind: a row or the bar may change once Yolo answers, and the loading screen a start showed for an item Yolo no longer resumes gives way to the home screen. The browser's storage holds titles and artwork links of what the owner watched there, beside the device credential it already held.
