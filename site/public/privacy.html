01Who owns it

Yours, not ours.

Each Yolo has one owner: the person who deployed it from the open-source code to their own Cloudflare account and home machine. The owner controls everything it stores. Yolo's developer, Haitian Liu, publishes the code and the apps, runs no Yolo for anyone, and cannot see into yours.

This policy covers every Yolo app: the player in a browser, in its car profile for a Tesla and its web profile for computers and phones; Yolo TV for Apple TV; and apps to come, such as one for iPhone. It also covers this website.

02Where it lives

Three places, all yours.

Your server
A Cloudflare Worker in your own Cloudflare account. Its database (D1) and logs (Workers Logs) belong to that account. Cloudflare hosts it for you under your agreement with Cloudflare and its privacy policy.
Your media node
Runs on your home machine. It reads your media, prepares streams, and runs the plugins you chose.
Each screen
Keeps only its own sign-in credential and a few display preferences, on the device itself.
03What is kept

What your server keeps, and why.

History and progress
So you can pick up where you left off, on any screen.
Queue, playlists, tracks
So what you lined up plays next, in the languages you prefer.
Paired screens
The name and type you gave each one, and a hash of its credential, so it can be recognized and removed at once.
Your passkeys
Public keys only, to sign you in to settings and to approve new screens.
Ask conversations
Your questions and the agent's short answers, to show and continue a conversation. Its reasoning and tool results are not kept. Conversations idle for 90 days are deleted.
What agents put on a screen
Notes, pictures, and choices your agent placed on a screen, until they are closed or expire (choices after 10 minutes), and what you picked or closed, kept for 24 hours so the agent can act on it.
Corrected matches
When you or your agent say which film or episode an item is, or that it is none, that choice, so the item keeps showing that way.
Share links
A hash of each link, its item, and how often it was played, so you can see and revoke them. Links expire after 7 days.
Playback diagnostics
From the browser player only, written to your Workers Logs to diagnose stalls and errors. Secrets are removed before sending. Never your voice, never your words.
04What is sent

What each app sends.

The player, in a car or any browser

To your server
Requests to browse, search, and play; playback reports (position, state, errors); the credential it received when paired; and the diagnostics above, including the browser's user agent, screen size, and media capabilities, so the right stream is chosen.
To your media node
Requests for video and subtitles, on your home network or by its remote address.
On the device
Its credential and display preferences, in the browser's local storage.

Yolo TV

To your server and node
The same browsing, playback, and progress requests. No diagnostics or analytics of its own. Every picture, subtitle, and video comes from your own server and node.
On the device
Your server's address and its credential, in the Apple TV's Keychain.

Apps to come

New apps speak the same protocol to your own server and follow this policy. If one would ever send anything not described here, this policy changes before that app ships.

05Local network

Your local network, for two things only.

Finding your server
Your media node announces your Yolo server on your home network with Bonjour (_yolo._tcp), so Yolo TV and Yolo's other Apple apps can offer it instead of asking you to type an address.
Playing at home
When the screen and the node share a network, video goes straight from one to the other, never out to the internet.

No other device on your network is scanned or reported.

06Ask and voice

Your voice is heard, never kept.

Ask hands a request, like “play the next episode”, to the AI agent you installed on your home machine, such as Claude Code or Codex. Your question goes to your server, then to that agent. The agent works under your own account with its provider, whose terms and privacy policy apply to what it processes.

In the browser
When your node has a transcription plugin, the player can record your request, after the browser asks you. The recording, 30 seconds at most, passes through your server to that plugin on your node, and only the words come back. It is never written to disk, logged, or stored. The plugin is one you chose and configured; it may run on your machine or call a speech service you set up.
On Apple TV
Yolo TV never records. Dictating with the Siri Remote uses Apple's own dictation, under Apple's privacy policy, and only the text reaches Yolo.
07Sharing

One video, for one friend, for seven days.

A share link plays one item in any browser for 7 days, without pairing. Your friend sees only that item. Nothing they do touches your history or progress, and they are not identified. Your server counts how often each link is played.

08Your choices

Plugins and agents you choose.

Your media node loads the plugins you configure: media sources, AI subtitles, transcription, agents, a model for the other plugins, and metadata. A plugin may contact the service it exists for; a source plugin, for example, fetches a video from the site it comes from. You can also connect an AI assistant to your server over MCP. Your server makes these connections under your accounts. The Yolo apps do not, and Yolo's developer is never involved.

Recognizing a video
To tell which film or episode a video is, your media node sends its title, description, uploader, duration, and file name to an AI model, never the video or your history. It goes through the Codex or Claude Code signed in on your home machine, or a subscription you signed Yolo in to, under your account with that provider, whose terms and privacy policy apply. Leave the model plugin out of your configuration to turn this off.
Posters and cast
To show a film's or an episode's posters, synopsis, and cast, your media node looks it up in Wikidata, Wikipedia, TVmaze, and Bangumi, sending the work's name (to Wikipedia, the article it found) and a season and episode number, never the video or your history. When you set a key of your own from The Movie Database, it searches there too; without a key, nothing is sent there. Leave the metadata plugins out of your configuration to turn this off.
09What we collect

Nothing.

The Yolo apps contain no analytics, crash reporting, or advertising code. They do not track you across apps or websites, and send nothing to Yolo's developer. If you allow it in your device's settings, Apple may share crash reports with developers, under Apple's privacy policy. This website is static, sets no cookies, loads nothing from anyone else, and counts no visits.

When you email support or open an issue on GitHub, we see what you send. Please leave out media tokens, credentials, and anything personal you would rather keep.

10Your control

In your hands.

  • Remove a screen in Yolo's settings: its credential stops working at once, and the screen returns to pairing.
  • Revoke any share link in Settings, under Share links.
  • Delete everything by deleting the D1 database and the Worker in your Cloudflare account, and Yolo's data on your home machine.
  • Deleting a Yolo app removes what it stored on that device. On Apple TV, also remove the screen in Yolo's settings, since the Keychain may keep its credential.
11The rest

Children, changes, questions.

Yolo is not directed to children. What plays is the owner's own media, by the owner's own choice.

Changes are published here with a new effective date. Every past version is in the public repository.

Write to support@useyolo.app, or open an issue on GitHub.
